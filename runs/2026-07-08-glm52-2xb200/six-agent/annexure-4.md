### 2. IMARC Group — Japan Rehabilitation Equipment Market (2025–2034)

**Headline market size:**
- **2025 (base year): USD 724.2 million**
- **2034 (forecast): USD 1,278.0 million** (~USD 1.28 billion)
- **CAGR 2026–2034: 6.51%**
- Historical period covered: 2020–2025.
- (An earlier edition of the same IMARC report put 2024 at USD 679.5 million growing to USD 1,261.2 million by 2033 at 6.58% CAGR — consistent series.)

**Rough JPY equivalent**: At ~¥150/USD, the Japan rehab-equipment market is on the order of **¥110 billion (2025) heading to ~¥190 billion (2034)** — a mid-sized device segment growing meaningfully faster (6.5%/yr) than the broader welfare-equipment market (~2–3%/yr, per JASPA).

**Segmentation:**

*By product type:*
1. **Daily living aids** — medical beds, bathroom/toilet assist devices, reading/writing aids
2. **Exercise equipment** — upper-body and lower-body training equipment
3. **Body support devices** — patient lifts, lifting slings
4. **Mobility equipment** — walking aids, wheelchairs and scooters

*By application:* physiotherapy; occupational therapy.

*By end user:* hospitals and clinics; rehabilitation centers; home care settings; physiotherapy centers.

**Growth drivers and trends:**
- **Aging-driven disease burden**: rising prevalence of chronic and non-communicable diseases among the elderly — cancer, arthritis, osteoporosis, Parkinson's disease, and neurological disorders — expands the rehabilitation caseload across hospitals, clinics, home care, and rehab centers.
- **Demand for better clinical outcomes**: providers upgrading to enhanced equipment.
- **Remote healthcare adoption**: tele-rehabilitation programs pull through home-use rehabilitation equipment.
- **Technology leadership**: Japan is an early adopter of robotic-assisted therapy, virtual reality rehabilitation, and tele-rehabilitation — these premium modalities raise average equipment spend.
- **R&D investment**: increasing device-maker R&D noted as a market accelerant.
- Government-backed accessibility initiatives support institutional procurement.

**Interpretation**: Rehabilitation equipment is the fastest-growing *device* niche in the elderly-care complex (6.5% CAGR vs ~2–3% for welfare equipment overall), driven by the medicalization of elderly care: recovery-phase rehab wards, kaigo-iryouin conversion (+97% segment growth in FY2020, Yano), and the policy emphasis on restoring/maintaining function (自立支援・重度化防止) embedded in recent kaigo fee revisions.

### 3. Cross-reference: Kaigo-Iryouin (care medical institutions) — the rehab/medical-care boundary (Yano, file 08)

The Yano LTCI Business Market Survey (FY2020) identifies **Care medical institutions service (介護医療院): +97.0% year-on-year** — explosive growth as Japan converts old long-term-care hospital beds into the new kaigo-iryouin category (medical + care hybrid facilities), directly relevant to the rehabilitation/medical-care boundary segment. This is the fastest-growing service segment in the LTCI business market, ahead of complex services (+21.8%), periodic-visit/on-demand home care nursing (+18.3%), and home nursing (+10.5%). The high-growth categories are all community-based, medically-integrated service types — consistent with the government's Community-based Integrated Care System (地域包括ケアシステム) policy push toward keeping heavier-needs elderly out of hospitals.

The four LTCI service segments each exceeding ¥1 trillion (FY2020): care welfare facility services/tokuyo ¥1,965.1B; care health facility services/rouken ¥1,346.0B; adult day care ¥1,285.1B; home-visit care ¥1,002.8B — together ~¥5.6 trillion inside a ~¥11 trillion total LTCI cost base.

### 4. Synthesis: Two distinct market definitions and their reconciliation

The rehabilitation medical-care/equipment space is measured under two non-overlapping scopes that must be quoted separately:

1. **Fuji Keizai "rehabilitation-related (rehab-tech)" market**: ¥10.2B (FY2022) → ¥13.9B (FY2025), +47.9% vs FY2021. Narrow scope: exercise-therapy equipment, independence-support/mobility/rehab-support robots, robotic prosthetics, VR/app digital rehab, functional-training support systems. The largest block is exercise-therapy rehabilitation equipment (¥5.7B FY2022 → ¥6.1B FY2025). The fastest-growing sub-segments are VR/app digital rehab and functional-training support systems, each forecast at 5.0x FY2021 by FY2025.

2. **IMARC "rehabilitation equipment" market**: USD 724.2M (2025) → USD 1,278.0M (2034), CAGR 6.51% (~¥110B → ~¥190B at ¥150/USD). Broader scope including conventional daily-living aids, exercise equipment, body support devices, and mobility equipment, across physiotherapy/occupational therapy applications and hospital/clinic/rehab-center/home-care end users.

The ~10x gap between the two (¥13.9B vs ~¥110B) reflects scope: IMARC counts the full installed-base device market (beds, wheelchairs, lifts, walking aids included), while Fuji Keizai isolates the newer therapy-technology categories (robotics, VR/apps, software systems) that are the high-growth frontier.

**Common growth drivers tied to aging demographics**: Both sources tie growth to the rising elderly disease burden (arthritis, osteoporosis, Parkinson's, neurological disorders, stroke recovery) and to policy incentives — the 2020 medical-fee revision's expanded rehabilitation add-on coverage for robot products (Fuji Keizai), the 自立支援・重度化防止 (independence-support / severe-condition-prevention) emphasis in kaigo fee revisions (IMARC), and the kaigo-iryouin conversion shifting care into medically-integrated settings (Yano). Tele-rehabilitation and home-rehab DX are emerging channels, with Fuji Keizai noting the home-rehab DX market is expected to take off from FY2030 onward.

**Forecast horizon**: Fuji Keizai's forecast runs to FY2025 (with a longer-dated note that home-rehab DX takes off from FY2030); IMARC's forecast runs to 2034 at 6.51% CAGR. The two are consistent in direction: the rehabilitation device/equipment niche is the fastest-growing device segment in the elderly-care complex, outpacing the ~2–3%/yr welfare-equipment market.

**Scale context:** The care-robot market is tiny relative to surrounding segments — "roughly 0.1–0.2% of the ¥1.5-trillion welfare equipment market and a rounding error against ¥11-trillion insured care services." Its significance is strategic (labor substitution) rather than volumetric, with fast percentage growth off a very small base.

**Product categories covered (5 functional domains):**
- **移乗支援 (transfer assistance)** — wearable (装着型) and non-wearable (非装着型) variants
- **移動支援 (mobility support)** — outdoor and indoor
- **排泄支援 (excretion/toilet assistance)** — including excretion-prediction robots (排泄予��)
- **入浴支援 (bathing assistance)**
- **見守り支援 (monitoring)** — facility-based and home-based sensing/watch-over systems (the practical adoption leader in facilities)

**Drivers and dynamics:**
- Growth is led by "new products reflecting user needs" (ユー��ーニーズを反映させた新製品が��引) — practical usability, not raw robotics capability, decides adoption.
- Strong categories: **wrap-type portable toilets for home use** and **excretion-prediction robots for facilities**; the pandemic-era stay-at-home shift boosted home-use products.
- Growth to 2025 expected to concentrate in **transfer, excretion, and monitoring** segments, as new entrants compete on functionality, usability, and price.
- Policy tailwind: the 2024 kaigo fee revision added productivity-improvement requirements/incentives, and MHLW's workforce strategy explicitly lists robots/ICT as retention-and-productivity measures; public subsidy programs (introduction subsidies via prefectures) underwrite much of facility demand.
- Caution note: earlier national visions (e.g., ~¥50B by around 2020) proved far too optimistic; actual adoption follows subsidy cycles and staffing pain.

**Adjacent tech niches (per Yano, for context):** care ICT ~¥35.0 billion; care DX ~¥54.6 billion (FY2024). Together with care robots (~¥2.2–3.6B), these form the small, fast-growing technology layer atop the mature welfare-equipment base.

### Cross-Segment Synthesis — The "Barbell" Structure of Elderly Care Equipment

The three sub-segments together reveal a barbell-shaped market structure:

- **Huge, mature, low-growth base:** Welfare equipment ¥1.52T (FY2021, +2.9% YoY), growing ~2–3%/year since FY2009, tracking the certified-care population. Dominated by personal-care equipment (¥472.7B) and communication devices (¥414.4B).
- **Small, fast-growing technology niches:** Care robots ¥2.175B (FY2021, +12.1%) → ¥3.625B (FY2025 forecast, ~1.7×); care ICT ~¥35B; care DX ~¥54.6B (FY2024). These are strategic/labor-substitution plays, not yet volumetric.
- **Consumables layer:** Care food ~¥120B (2024) → ¥140.5B (2030), with home-channel liquid food and nutritional supplements as the growth vectors.

The LTCI-covered slice of welfare equipment (~¥121B) is small relative to the total ¥1.52T, meaning most equipment revenue is out-of-pocket/institutional — a different demand dynamic from the insurance-driven services market. The care-robot segment's significance is strategic (labor substitution responding to the care-workforce shortage) rather than volumetric, and its forecasts should be treated cautiously given historically over-optimistic national visions.

## Japan's Long-Term Care Insurance (LTCI / 介護保険) System & Nursing Care Services Market — Overall Size, Segment Breakdown, and Trend Analysis

### 1. The authoritative public-system figure: MHLW FY2023 LTCI Status Report (~¥11.7 trillion/year)

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's FY2023 (Reiwa 5) annual LTCI Business Status Report is the definitive measure of Japan's public nursing-care services market. Key figures (source: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/topics/kaigo/osirase/jigyo/23/dl/r05_point.pdf):

- **Total cost amount (費用額, includes user copayments): ¥11.7186 trillion in FY2023**, up ¥340.8 billion (+3.0% YoY) from ¥11.3778 trillion in FY2022.
- **Benefit expenditure (給付費, excludes user copayments): ¥10.83 trillion (¥108,263 hundred-million) in FY2023**, +¥316.3 billion (+3.0%) YoY. Benefit spending has grown **3.3x since FY2000** (¥3.24T), a long-run CAGR of ~5.4%, moderating to ~2–3%/year in the 2020s.

**Demand-side drivers (FY2023):**
- Category-1 insured persons (65+): 35.89 million (end of March 2024), with the late-elderly 75+ group (≈20.18 million) now dominating the insured base.
- Persons certified as requiring long-term care/support (要介護・要支援認定者): **7.08 million** at end of March 2024 (+140,000, +2.0% YoY), up from 2.56 million in FY2000 — a 2.8x increase in 23 years.
- Certification rate (share of 65+ certified): 19.4% in FY2023 (+0.4 pp); 75+ band ~31% (≈1 in 3 late-elderly certified).
- Service recipients (monthly average): 6.09 million in FY2023 (+1.7% YoY), split into home/at-home services 4.22 million (69.2%), community-based 0.91 million (15.0%), facility services 0.96 million (15.8%).

**Structural shift in benefit setting (FY2023 monthly average ��¥856.3 billion):** home services ¥438.4B (51.2%), community-based ¥146.7B (17.1%), facility services ¥271.2B (31.7%). Since FY2000 the facility share fell from 66% to ~32% while home + community-based rose to ~68% — a policy-driven shift toward aging-in-place. Facility services remain the most expensive per user (¥260–370k/month) versus home-visit care ~¥80k and day care ~¥84k.

### 2. Tricare Topps industry analysis — the ¥11-trillion Kaigo market

The Tricare Topps industry analysis (source: https://www.tricare.jp/knowledge/category4/category4_1/3450/) confirms the official cost trend from a private-sector vantage:

| Fiscal year | Total cost (Tricare series) |
|---|---|
| FY2019 | ¥10.51 trillion |
| FY2020 | ¥10.78 trillion |
| FY2021 | ¥11.03 trillion |
| FY2022 | ¥11.19 trillion |
| **FY2023** | **¥11.51 trillion** (+¥321 billion, +2.9% YoY) |

- Cumulative growth: **+¥1 trillion over 4 years (2019→2023)**, steady ~2–3%/year expansion.
- **Important reconciliation note:** the Tricare series (¥11.51T FY2023) differs slightly from the MHLW "point" document's ¥11.72T "because of differing inclusion of high-cost care service reimbursements; both series show the same trend." The MHLW status report's ¥11.7186 trillion total cost is the broader figure (includes user copayments); Tricare's ¥11.51T is a slightly narrower cost-base definition. Both confirm a market above ¥11 trillion.

**Supply-side constraints flagged:** care-sector effective job-openings-to-applicants ratio (FY2023) = **3.63x** vs 1.13x all-industry average; annual staff turnover 14.3%; projected care-worker shortage ~970,000 positions by 2040. Leading private care operators' FY2023 revenues ranged ¥88.7 billion to ¥268.9 billion — the market is highly fragmented, with even the largest operator holding only a low-single-digit share of the ¥11T+ market. Growth segments flagged: home-care, day services, paid elderly housing, plus peripheral markets (care food, equipment rental, DX tools).

### 3. TechSci Research — commercial "elderly care services" market (USD framing)

TechSci Research (source: https://www.techsciresearch.com/news/19664-japan-elderly-care-services-market.html) sizes the commercial elderly-care services market on a revenue/service-provider basis:

- **Market value (2024, base year): USD 11.77 billion** for Japan elderly care services.
- **Forecast CAGR: 7.49% over 2026–2030**, projecting **~USD 18.17 billion by 2030**.

**Segmentation by service type:** Home care services (health + non-medical, aligned with aging-in-place policy); Adult day care services (identified as the second dominant segment); Institutional care services (nursing homes, hospital-based care, assisted living, independent senior living). Regional split: Kansai (Osaka/Kyoto/Kobe) holds the second-dominant position after Kanto/Tokyo.

**Critical reconciliation — public-system vs commercial-market definitions:** The TechSci USD 11.77B (2024) figure is roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the public LTCI cost base (~¥11+ trillion �� USD 75+ billion). This is NOT a contradiction — it reflects different scoping conventions. The kaigo insurance figure counts total benefit spend across the insured system (a public-finance measure), while commercial market-research sizings capture defined service-provider revenue segments. The TechSci source explicitly warns: "this is a private-research definition of 'elderly care services' (revenue-based, service-provider view)... Both framings are needed in the report; do not conflate them." The 7.49% CAGR is a provider-revenue growth rate, not the public-benefit growth rate (~2–3%/year), reflecting commercial capture of out-of-pocket, peripheral, and technology-enabled services growing faster than the regulated insurance core.

### 4. Yano Research — LTCI business market segment breakdown (FY2020)

Yano Research Institute (source: https://www.yano.co.jp/press/press.php/002998; survey period Nov 2021–March 2022) provides the authoritative segment-level breakdown of insured nursing-care service spend. **Four segments each exceeded ¥1 trillion in FY2020:**

| Service segment | English | FY2020 market size |
|---|---|---|
| 介護福祉施設サービス | Care welfare facility services (special nursing homes / tokuyo) | ¥1,965.1 billion (¥1.97T) |
| 介護保健施設サービス | Care health facility services (geriatric health facilities / rouken) | ¥1,346.0 billion (¥1.35T) |
| 通所介護 | Adult day care (commuting care) | ¥1,285.1 billion (¥1.29T) |
| ���問介護 | Home-visit care | ¥1,002.8 billion (¥1.00T) |

These four alone total roughly **¥5.6 trillion**, inside a total LTCI cost base of ~¥11 trillion (FY2020) — i.e., facility care plus day care plus home-visit care are the volume core of Japan's nursing-care services market.

**Fastest-growing segments (YoY):**
- **Care medical institutions service (介護医療院): +97.0%** — explosive growth as Japan converts old long-term-care hospital beds into the new kaigo-iryouin category (medical + care hybrid facilities), directly relevant to the rehabilitation/medical-care boundary.
- Complex services (複合型サービス): +21.8%
- Periodic-visit/on-demand home care nursing (定期巡回・随時対応型訪問介護看護): +18.3%
- Home nursing (訪問看護): +10.5%

The high-growth categories are all community-based, medically-integrated service types — consistent with the government's Community-based Integrated Care System (地域包括ケアシステム) policy push toward keeping heavier-needs elderly out of hospitals.

**Workforce projections cited:** ~2.33 million care workers needed by 2023, 2.43 million by 2025, 2.80 million by 2040 (MHLW's 9th-plan update later put FY2040 need at ~2.72 million — order of magnitude consistent).

### 5. Long-run trend series and growth rates (MHLW finance slides)

The MHLW finance slide set (source: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/zaiseinoshikumi.pdf) gives the complete total-cost time series since system launch:

| FY | Total cost | of which benefits |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 | ¥3.6T | — |
| 2005 | ¥6.4T | — |
| 2010 | ¥8.0T | ¥7.8T |
| 2015 | ¥10.0T | ¥9.8T |
| 2019 | ¥11.4T | ¥10.8T |
| 2020 | ¥11.6T | ¥11.1T |
| 2021 | ¥11.8T | ¥11.3T |
| 2022 | ¥11.9T | ¥11.4T |

- **Total cost grew ~3.3x in 22 years (¥3.6T → ¥11.9T)**, a long-run CAGR of ~5.6%, decelerating to ~1–2%/year in the most recent years as fee revisions restrain unit prices even while user numbers rise.
- **FY2024 budget (Reiwa 6): care benefit spend ¥13.2 trillion; total cost base ¥14.2 trillion** — the forward budget envelope is larger than the FY2022/FY2023 actuals, signaling continued escalation.
- **65+ average monthly premium** more than doubled from ¥2,911 (1st period, 2000–2002) to ¥6,225 (9th period, 2024–2026); the political ceiling on premium growth is a key reason fee revisions hold service-price growth below demand growth.

### 6. International comparison (GoLTC profile)

The Global Observatory of Long-Term Care (source: https://goltc.org/system-profile/japan) places Japan's public LTC spending in international context: USD 32.7 billion at system launch (2000) → USD 106.4 billion in 2019 (≈2% of GDP) → USD 136.4 billion projected by 2025. Growth 2000–2019 was +226%, among the fastest-expanding public LTC budgets in the OECD. This confirms the nursing-care services market at roughly ¥11–12 trillion/year (USD ~110–135B), 2% of GDP and rising, with a demand curve locked in by demographics and a supply curve constrained by a ~30% underpaid, shrinking workforce.

### 7. Reconciliation summary — public-system vs commercial market definitions

| Source / framing | Scope | FY2023/2024 figure | Growth framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| MHLW FY2023 Status Report (total cost, incl. copay) | Public insured system, all settings | ¥11.7186 trillion (+3.0% YoY) | ~2–3%/year recently; 3.3x since FY2000 |
| MHLW FY2023 benefit expenditure | Public benefits (excl. copay) | ¥10.83 trillion (+3.0% YoY) | long-run CAGR ~5.4%, decelerating |
| Tricare Topps (industry analysis) | MHLW cost base, narrower inclusion | ¥11.51 trillion FY2023 (+2.9%) | +¥1T over 4 years |
| MHLW FY2024 budget envelope | Forward budget | ¥13.2T benefits / ¥14.2T total | — |
| Yano LTCI business survey (FY2020) | Segment-level insured spend | 4 segments >¥1T, totaling ~¥5.6T inside ~¥11T base | facility core + home/community growth |
| TechSci Research (commercial) | Service-provider revenue, USD | USD 11.77B (2024) → ~USD 18.17B (2030) | 7.49% CAGR (2026–2030) |
| GoLTC (international, USD) | Public LTC spending | USD 106.4B (2019) → USD 136.4B (2025 proj.) | +226% 2000–2019 |

**Key reconciliation insight:** The ~¥11.7 trillion public LTCI cost base and the ~USD 11.77 billion (≈¥1.8T at ~¥150/USD) commercial services market differ by roughly an order of magnitude because they measure different things — the public figure is total system benefit spend (a finance/entitlement measure), while the commercial figure is provider revenue within defined service segments (excluding much of the public benefit pass-through and the facility room/board self-pay). The public system is growing ~2–3%/year (restrained by fee revisions and premium ceilings), while the commercial services market is forecast to grow ~7.5%/year, capturing faster-growing out-of-pocket, peripheral, and technology-enabled segments. The segment mix is shifting from facility care (66% → ~32% share since FY2000) toward home + community-based services (~68%), with the fastest-growing categories (kaigo-iryouin +97%, complex services +21.8%, periodic-visit home nursing +18.3%) all being community-based, medically-integrated types aligned with the Community-based Integrated Care System policy.
